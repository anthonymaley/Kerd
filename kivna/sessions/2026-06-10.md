## Key Decisions

- **switch is session-first, not machine-first.** The two cases produce identical artifacts, so this was a framing change with no behavior change. The trigger now leads with session handoff and adds `save context`.
- **dian doesn't own the session boundary.** switch owns open (context load) and close (session log, vault save, commit). dian owns the disciplined middle (task-framing, plan-approval, execute discipline). Codified in `state-contract.md`: switch is the sole caller of `kivna save`.
- **Keep dian's gates, don't strip them.** They duplicate the user's global CLAUDE.md gates but are load-bearing for anyone installing Kerd without that global file. Made self-contained instead of stripped.
- **Selective Strunk borrowing for skriv, not wholesale pairing.** elements-of-style agrees with skriv on concreteness but conflicts on sentence-uniformity and topic-sentence structure — wholesale pairing would homogenize voice.

## Insights

- The dian change was a **boundary-sharpening, not a deletion.** dian and switch had blurry overlap (both loaded context, both touched the vault). Pulling the line clean made dian smaller *and* the whole system easier to reason about. The `state-contract.md` edit is the load-bearing part — it's the single source of truth that stops the two skills drifting back into overlap.
- **"Duplication" is relative to the environment.** A rule that's redundant for this user (who has global gates) is essential for a stranger who installs the plugin cold. Recommendations have to be calibrated to shipped-product vs personal-use, and those can point in opposite directions.
- The user's real switch usage (exit at 60% to preserve clarity, never compact) means switch-out is **not** token-starved — which killed the "offload synthesis to a subagent" idea I'd brought in. The discipline of switching early is itself the optimization.
