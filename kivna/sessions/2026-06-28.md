## Key Decisions

- **A mode is a right-sizing contract, not an orchestration checklist.** Skills are everyday tools; a mode exists only to stop over/under-cooking process for a shape of work. Most "business modes" (legal/sales/writing/research/strategy/maintain) are tool-use, not modes.
- **Kerd owns workflow-shaped modes; it is not a router over external skills.** GSD removed; Superpowers distrusted as waterfall. This kills the 2026-05-04 redesign's core premise (it's superseded).
- **Three altitudes:** switch (boundary) / coach (= renamed `dian`, session conductor) / dial (lifecycle PM walking explore→validate→plan→build→launch). They nest, don't compete.
- **JIT is two things:** the build-philosophy (Build stage) AND the universal decision-style (the focus/partner-mode). The rigor dial rises with stages (more depth), but ceremony/noise stay low and the decision-style stays JIT throughout. spike = Explore stage; jit = Build stage.
- **Question style:** no X/Y binaries — Tony answers only the first and it confuses. Use open/confirm shapes. It's the format, not the asking — engage freely.
- **Adopt no external memory tool** — git-portable + human-first + full-fidelity pickup is deliberate and worth keeping.

## Insights

- **The design dogfooded its own predecessors.** We used jit (slices + eyeball gates) and partner-mode (one decision at a time, low noise) to design the dial that will absorb them. The method validated itself: a full architecture + 5-stage spec emerged from short exchanges, no big solo dump.
- **The altitude jump came from asking what a mode FAILS to do, not what it does.** Reframing "mode = a ruleset that prevents mis-sizing process" collapsed ~15 work-domains into ~5 shapes, then into one staged lifecycle with a rigor dial. The right question dissolved most of the apparent complexity.
