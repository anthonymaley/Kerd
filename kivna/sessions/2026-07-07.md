## Key Decisions

- **Delegated execution is an explicit toggle, not auto-detection** — `/kerd:conductor fable on|off`, off by default, session-scoped. Rejected the auto-detect gating: conductor can't reliably know its own model, and the real constraint is credit availability (session-variable), which the user asserts by flipping the toggle. Inline on the active model stays the default.
- **The spec is the contract** — a vague `[delegate]` step yields a confidently-wrong build from a cheaper model with no recourse, so spec quality is the safety mechanism and the whole justification for spending the expensive planning tokens.
- **Two step-tags, two dispatch modes** — tags are `[fable]`/`[delegate]` (actual implementer model picked at dispatch, not baked into the tag); dispatch is in-session subagents (default) or fresh-session handoff, chosen per session because Tony picked "both" at the design fork.
- **Top-level capability list left unchanged** — "session discipline" subsumes delegation; whether to surface model-tiered delegation as its own capability is logged as an open question in CONTEXT.md rather than decided.

## Insights

- **Delegation didn't need a new protocol** — it slotted into conductor's existing plan-approval gate. The spec-as-contract discipline is just conductor's existing "ban vague plan items" rule turned up to spec-grade, and the dispatch fork rides on the plan/execute split already there. The change is additive, not structural.
- **Building this inline (not delegated) was itself the correct call** — skill design is `[fable]`-type judgment work, exactly what the opt-in gate says *not* to delegate. Good confirmation the gate is scoped right: the feature's own construction is a negative example for it.
