## Gotchas

- **`## Criteria`, `## Options`, `## Evaluation matrix` and `## Countermeasures`
  must be TABLE-ONLY.** Prose after a table parses as rows; prose *before* one
  parses as the header. Same family as the risk-ledger gotcha.
- **The matrix parser's `_split_row` is a naive `split("|")`** — `\|` is not
  honoured, so an escaped pipe in a cell splits the row. Hit live: a
  countermeasure row became eight columns. A block format has no pipe problem,
  which is part of why the register uses one.
- **`collision_report` treats a box under 420×120 as "small"** and flags any
  free text overlapping it. Bound text is exempt. Drawing a glyph *over* a cell
  scores a fault; binding it *to* the cell does not.
- **A block parser must stop at `## ` as well as `### `.** Without it, the last
  block in each section absorbs the following heading into its body — six false
  hash divergences on the register's first parse.
- **An agent told "STRICTLY READ-ONLY, never modify anything under ROOT" still
  wrote `output/` into the repo** (StrictDoc's cache, 10:32). Prompt-layer
  instructions to subagents are not a sandbox.

## Insights

- **Three rounds of "are you sure?" produced three DIFFERENT failures**, and no
  single check would have caught more than one. Round 1: findings never reached
  the builder, because an agent ran in parallel with the study and was told not
  to wait. Round 2: findings reached a prose summary that kept the conclusions
  and dropped the mechanisms. Round 3: findings reached me but *their
  refutations did not* — I consumed the claims and skipped the corrections. The
  producer had to ask each time; the check never fired on its own.
- **The verification pass's own conclusion is the strongest argument for the
  register.** Forty findings lived in a transcript, so "did I carry all of them"
  was a memory test dressed as a gate. Give findings IDs and it becomes a count.
  The item's thesis, demonstrated by the thing building the fix failing it twice.
- **`final` cannot catch compression.** The producer corrected `UX-001` at 21:23
  — it was scoped to the evaluation matrix only, and I had filed it as a general
  rule. The hash proves words have not changed since approval; it cannot prove
  they were the right words *at* approval. Only he could catch that, and only by
  reading it back. What the register buys is not correctness but **legibility**.
- **Six of twenty category definitions were deleted rather than patched**,
  because each rejected its own category's rows. `PRIV`'s question returned yes
  for all fifty. `STA`'s made the category unfillable. They read perfectly well.
- **The measurement that opened the session got worse, honestly.** It began with
  "28 requirements produced, zero promoted". It closes at 50 produced — and now
  there is somewhere to promote them to.
