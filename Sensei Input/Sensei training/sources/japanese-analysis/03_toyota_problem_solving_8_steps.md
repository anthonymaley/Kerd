# Toyota Problem-Solving 8 Steps (トヨタ式問題解決 ８ステップ) + A3 Thinking (A3思考)

**Status of the source:** Toyota Business Practices (TBP) is an **internal training program** — the full booklet is not publicly sold. The **8-step structure** is widely taught across Japanese industry, appears in Toyota Enterprise Inc.'s public seminar materials, and is documented in secondary literature (especially John Shook's *Managing to Learn*, 2008, and Liker 2004). What follows is the publicly-attested framework in Japanese with translation and nuance.

---

## 問題解決 ８ステップ — The Eight Steps

### 原文 (as taught in Japanese industry, widely reproduced)

| ステップ | 原文 | 意味 |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | **問題の明確化** (mondai no meikakuka) | Clarify the problem |
| Step 2 | **問題の層別と問題点の特定** (sōbetsu to tokutei) | Break down the problem; isolate the specific point |
| Step 3 | **目標の設定** (mokuhyō no settei) | Set the target (value + deadline) |
| Step 4 | **真因の追究** (shin'in no tsuikyū) — なぜなぜ分析 | Pursue the root cause — via 5-whys |
| Step 5 | **対策立案** (taisaku ritsuan) | Draft countermeasures |
| Step 6 | **対策の実行** (taisaku no jikkō) | Execute the countermeasures |
| Step 7 | **効果の確認** (kōka no kakunin) | Verify the effect |
| Step 8 | **標準化と横展開** (hyōjunka to yokotenkai) | Standardize and deploy horizontally |

### 英訳 (my rendering)

1. Clarify the problem
2. Break the problem down and pinpoint the specific issue (the *problem-point*, 問題点)
3. Set the target (both the value you're aiming for and when you'll hit it)
4. Pursue the true cause (真因, *shin'in*) — through repeated "why"
5. Formulate countermeasures and a schedule
6. Execute them
7. Check effect and evaluate
8. Standardize the successful countermeasure and spread it horizontally across other lines/plants

### ニュアンス・ギャップ分析

- **問題 vs 問題点.** Japanese distinguishes between 問題 (the broad problem) and 問題点 (the specific, isolated point of the problem). English often collapses these into "problem." Step 2's whole purpose is the move from the broad to the narrow — **問題 → 問題点**. English loses this by using one word.
- **真因 vs 原因.** Japanese distinguishes 原因 (*gen'in*, cause — any cause) from 真因 (*shin'in*, *true/root* cause). The kanji 真 means "true." The 5-whys output isn't just "a cause" but the *true* cause. English "root cause" is close, but 真因 is morally stronger — it implies there is a *truth* to uncover, and lesser causes are *falsehoods* masquerading as explanations.
- **横展開 (yokotenkai, "horizontal deployment").** This is a Toyota-specific cultural habit that English lean often misses. *A countermeasure that works in one place must be actively spread to every place with the same risk.* It's not "share best practices" (passive); it's *spread sideways* (active, almost military). The word 横 (side/horizontal) + 展開 (deploy/unfold) is an imperative: *take the solution and lay it out across the whole organization*.
- **Step 8 is the step most English versions shortchange.** Once a fix works, Western teams tend to declare victory. Toyota teams standardize it (prevent regression) and deploy it sideways (prevent isolated knowledge). Missing either half means the gain decays.
- **The sequence is a causal discipline.** Skip Step 1 and you fix the wrong thing. Skip Step 2 and you generalize too broadly. Skip Step 3 and you can't tell if you succeeded. Skip Step 4 and you're playing whack-a-mole. The steps are not a checklist — they are a *discipline for not fooling yourself*.

## A3思考 (A3 Thinking) / A3報告書 (A3 Report)

### 原文

**A3資料** or **A3報告書** — a single sheet of A3 paper (roughly 11" × 17") on which the entire problem-solving narrative is told. The format isn't Toyota-proprietary — A3 reports descend from QC-story (QCストーリー) conventions in Japanese quality management — but Toyota enforces it as a universal thinking discipline.

Typical layout:

- 左側 (left side): 現状把握 (grasp of current state) → 問題の層別 → 目標設定
- 右側 (right side): 真因追究 → 対策 → 実施計画 → 確認 → 標準化・横展開

### 意味

The A3 is a **thinking tool disguised as a report format.** It is a *kata* (型, a practiced form) for thinking through a problem. The size constraint forces editing. The left-to-right flow enforces the 8-step order. The visual layout forces causation to be made explicit.

### ニュアンス・ギャップ分析

- **Format = discipline.** Western business documents are often judged on content. A3 thinking judges *form as substance* — if your A3 doesn't flow left-to-right, you haven't thought clearly yet.
- The Japanese term 型 (*kata*, "form/pattern") applies here. Like a kata in martial arts, the A3 is a **form you practice repeatedly to internalize the underlying discipline**. A3 is not about producing a document; it's about producing a person who thinks that way. Mike Rother's *Toyota Kata* builds on this.
- **報告書 (report) vs 相談書 (consultation).** In practice, a Toyota A3 is often less a finished report and more a *conversation* with one's supervisor (上司, *jōshi*). It exists to be marked up, questioned, iterated. This is closer to *Managing to Learn* (Shook 2008), where the A3 is a **coaching document between mentor and mentee**. English usage often treats A3 as a deliverable; Japanese usage treats it as a dialogue medium.

## Connected concept: 標準作業 (Standardized Work)

Toyota's Step 8 hinges on 標準作業 — standardized work. The 3-element formulation (公開されている定義):

1. **タクトタイム** (takt time, タクト < German *Takt*, beat/pulse) — the rhythm of customer demand
2. **作業順序** (*sagyō junjo*) — the sequence of operations
3. **標準手持ち** (*hyōjun temochi*) — the standard work-in-process (WIP) needed to run the sequence

### ニュアンス

標準 (*hyōjun*) means *standard* in the sense of **the current best-known method, which is the baseline to improve from**. This is different from "standard" in English business usage, which often connotes a *fixed* rule. In Toyota Japanese, *every standard is a temporary benchmark awaiting the next kaizen*. The phrase commonly attributed to Ohno (paraphrased by secondary sources) is: *where there is no standard, there is no kaizen* — standards aren't restrictions, they're the foundation that makes improvement possible and visible.

## How these fit together

```
                              徹底的なムダの排除
                              (mission: thoroughly eliminate waste)
                                        │
                       ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
                       ▼                                 ▼
             ジャスト・イン・タイム                    自働化
              (demand-pull)                        (halt on abnormality)
                       │                                 │
                       └────────────────┬────────────────┘
                                        ▼
                             問題の見える化
                           (make problems visible)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                              問題解決 ８ステップ
                                   (TBP, A3)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                           標準作業 + 横展開
                     (standardize + spread sideways)
                                        │
                                        └── back to 改善 (continuous loop)
```

The point: tools (kanban, SMED, poka-yoke, andon, etc.) are instrumental to *making problems visible*. Once visible, the 8-step/A3 discipline resolves them. Standardization locks the gain; 横展開 spreads it. The loop is closed by 改善 — never finished, always tightening.

**Where English adopters usually fall short:**
1. They implement tools without the mission (徹底的なムダの排除) and without the surfacing-visibility step.
2. They treat 5-whys as a blame inquiry rather than *自問自答*.
3. They skip Step 8 (standardize + 横展開) and lose their gains.
4. They implement "automation" without **自働化**'s halt-on-abnormality principle.
