# Tim Platt — TMMNA Pocketmod (1998-2001)

Toyota training material documented by Tim Platt, TMMNA Information Systems. A pocket-sized reference he kept on his person and gave to a select few. Platt came from TMMNA to Toyota Europe for several years; this document was part of his knowledge transfer.

The content is Toyota's — Platt documented and compiled it, he did not create the methodologies. The story types, nemawashi process, and TBP steps are Toyota Way, not Platt's personal framework. Same attribution principle as Liker: the author recorded Toyota's thinking, Toyota owns the thinking.

Source: Physical pocketmod PDF, 8 pages. Not a published book — internal operational material.

## 8 Components of Nemawashi

"The process of cross-organizational communication and consensus building."

### Relationships (1-4)

1. **Be Personable** — Be personable. Be friendly. Say "Hello." Speak to people in the halls. Be "a part" of the organization.

2. **Build Partnerships** — Build personal relationships with a select number of people. Converse with them regularly about both personal and work topics.

3. **Be Proactive** — Be proactive and identify the key stakeholders for specific activities. Map stakeholders across H/R, Tech Planning, A/F.

4. **Build Pyramid** — Understand the "formal" organization and hierarchy and develop an understanding of the "informal" organization or layers and who has an established relationship.

### Communication (5-8)

5. **Be Provider** — Stay in touch with key stakeholders. Keep them informed of progress or activities. Share information with them.

6. **Begin with Peers** — When building consensus, start with your peers across the organization and spiral up through the layers. Return to a lower level if necessary. As you start to gain consensus, others will become involved and use their partnerships.

7. **Break for Preview** — Have preliminary informal discussions, hand sketching concept and story if necessary.

8. **Bare Prospects** — Outline for informal discussions:
   a) Review the history
   b) Identify previous reviewers
   c) Relay current thoughts/alternatives
   d) Ask questions / Get Feedback
   e) Adjust thoughts
   f) Repeat process with others

### Benefits of Nemawashi
- No Surprises
- Collective Knowledge
- Give & Take
- Each one is a person, not an enemy

## 7 A3 Story Types

Pre-Requisite: Determine the intended audience. This will drive the "level" of the story that is possible and appropriate.

### Information Stories

**1. Educate to the Details**
Structure: Simplest Level → Medium Level of Detail → Detailed Level
Use when: You need to educate them on the details, but need to begin with a higher level in order to provide the bigger picture first.

**2. Illumination of the Unknown**
Structure: Known → Unknown
Use when: You need to share information or when you need to educate someone. You start with what they know and then fill in the details.

**3. Compare & Contrast**
Structure: Current Situation → New Situation
Use when: You want to compare and contrast the way a situation is now versus what it will look like after some change has occurred.

**7. Education/Satisfaction**
Structure: Introduction (Purpose, Background) → Point 1 → Point 2 → ... → Point n → Conclusion (Summary, Benefits)
Use when: You need to educate the audience on a series of related topics.

### Recommendation Stories

**4. The Standard Toyota Proposal**
Structure: Current Situation/Background → Problems & Cause → Proposal & Benefits
Use when: A proposal is being made to improve the condition as a result of reducing problems or gaining benefits not currently available.

**5. Correcting Discrepancy from Standard**
Structure: Current Situation → Standards & Discrepancy → Counter-measures
Use when: The current situation differs from the standard that has been established and countermeasure activity is in order.

**6. Develop the Roadmap**
Structure: Trends & Analysis → Strategic Direction (roadmap)
Use when: You have identified the appropriate trends, analyzed their impact on the organization, and decided upon an appropriate strategic direction.

### 5-Step A3 Story Creation Process

1. **Select the basic outline** — pick the story type from the list above
2. **Hand write each box** — develop 3-7 key and important bullet points for each box of the story. Be sure to develop at the level of your intended audience.
3. **Navigate the "Rock Solid" principles** — Tests that will sink your ship:
   - If it's too much to handwrite, you have too much
   - The audience should know why you're doing what you're reading
   - Someone who has no idea (president, grandparent, etc.) should understand
   - 3 minute elevator ride
4. **Illustrate the story through charts or pictures** — Make the illustrations as simple as the audience requires. Don't be concerned about achieving 100% completeness. You're trying to relay a story, not build a skyscraper.
5. **Nemawashi and Refine** — Review your story with others to see if it makes sense to them and if they understand. Try on a friend.

## ROOKIES — Characteristics of a Respected Leader

**R**ational — calm, relaxed demeanor; say it with a smile; avoid "you" when expressing negative aspects; embrace logic, avoid personal negative emotion

**O**pen — rarely say "No", always consider alternatives; express known advantages & disadvantages; try to consider the other perspective; set expectations upfront

**O**ptimistic — extol the positive aspects; remind others of solid plan; remind others of benefits of problems

**K**nowledgeable — never stop at "I don't know", seek answers; don't blame staff for knowledge you should have; use Internet and knowledgeable resources; identify total scope

**I**ntegrity-Bound — tell the truth tactfully; don't twist the facts; admit your mistakes and apologize

**E**ven-Handed — be fair with staff members; be fair to other divisions

**S**ure — be confident or commit to finding out; seek out necessary knowledge; if you don't know, ask

## Characteristics of Successful Projects

### Organization
1. Clear visible leader leading core group with executive sponsorship
2. Clear responsibilities/roles/ownership (Lead vs. Support matrix per function)
3. Process Methodology
4. Cost Management Methodology (Budget → Ringi → authorize)

### Management and Visual Control

"Use as a tool, not just as documentation!!"

Tiered PDCA structure:
- **Executive Mgmt** (periodically): Issues tracking (item, impact, current, responsible, countermeasure, target) and Things to Watch
- **Project Mgr / Cross Divisional** (ongoing): Schedule + milestones, PDCA by group (priorities & strategy → targets → current activity → eval → current condition → countermeasures)
- **Activity Managers** (weekly): Same PDCA structure + requests for other groups
- **Performers of Tasks** (daily): Tasks, responsibilities, gantt chart

## Toyota Business Practices (TBP) — 8 Steps

1. Clarify the problem
2. Break down the problem
3. Target Setting
4. Root Cause Analysis
5. Develop countermeasures
6. See countermeasures through
7. Monitor both results and processes
8. Standardise successful processes

### Drive and Dedication
- Customer First
- Always confirm the purpose of your work
- Ownership and responsibility
- Visualisation (Mieruka)
- Judgment based upon facts
- Think and act persistently
- Follow each process with sincerity and commitment
- Speedy action in a timely manner
- Thorough Communication
- Involve all stakeholders

### Base Toyota Way (CGRRT)
1. **Challenge (C)**: delivery, challenge, long-range perspective, thorough consideration
2. **Kaizen (K)**: kaizen mind, innovation, lean systems, structure, org. learning
3. **Genchi-Genbutsu (G)**: genchi-genbutsu, consensus building, commitment
4. **Respect (R)**: respect, mutual trust and responsibility, sincere communication
5. **Teamwork (T)**: development, respect individual, consolidate power as team

### Foundational Expectations for All
1. Think Deeply considering others' perspective practically. (C, T)
2. Implement Structured Processes that produce repeatable results. (K, T)
3. Build consensus and communicate through application of Nemawashi principles. (G, R)
4. Consider how to best apply efforts to achieve 80% of total benefit with 20% of the effort (80/20 rule) for each phase of each activity. (K)
5. Always, Always, Always bring Backup Documentation to meetings, esp. proposal reviews. (G)
