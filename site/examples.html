Real work, not invented.

Three pieces of work done with Kerd, each set out as a programme: the piece, the seats that played, and what happened. A seat its record does not show playing stays dark.

Every line here comes from the full record, which is one link away on each. Point at a name in the cast to find its seat.

Eight sittings over two days, 2 and 3 September 2026.

MusicTUI, across sittings.

The piece
MusicTUI is a terminal client for Apple Music. Its cleanup could delete the playlist the app had just created to play from. The fix was one row of work, “Discover lifecycle serialisation”, carried by name from the sitting that found it to the sitting that shipped it.
What happened

Codex found it during a review of something else, and the close wrote it down with its mechanism and one constraint: plan before code. The next four sittings did other work, a push failure, a merge, a release, and each one carried the row forward.

When it was picked up, the owner held it to a design: three review rounds with Codex, four drafts, nothing built. On his go it was built, reviewed by Codex and gated live. The gate passed, and also turned up one deletion nobody could explain. The close wrote it down in full, two timestamps two seconds apart, and the next sitting built a measuring campaign on that detail before v3.12.0 went out.

What the record does not show
Anyone in the act of reading a log at pickup. And the deletion was never explained: the work closed the races in the app’s own code, but did not make the symptom impossible.

Kerd 0.134.0, released 17 September 2026. Two review rounds.

A reviewed release.

The piece
A correction to Kerd’s Visuals skill, the illustrator’s own part. The owner brought two versions of the same diagram card, one in implementation terms and one in product language, and asked for the difference to be written in as a contract correction, not a new governance protocol. Two clauses were new: an expansion test, and the project or repository name readable inside the picture.
What happened

A Claude session wrote it, and Codex, which did not write it, read it before the push. Five findings across two rounds, all five accepted. The first hit the very rule the release existed to add: as written, a whole class of factual view had no way to pass it.

The fix for that was reviewed again and had the same flaw one level up: it let an author choose which test their own view takes. Codex’s wording replaced it, so content decides the test, never the title. 731 tests and a clean gate were green at every one of those points. None of them caught either flaw.

What the record does not show
That the two new clauses work. They had not been measured on real diagram output when the release shipped, and the release said so.

One evening and the next morning. The first real use of rehearsal and the concert.

This package.

The piece
The README, the website, the guides, the pictures and the other two examples, made as one piece of work. The record is a self-report by the conducting session; where someone else checked something, it says who.
What happened

Rehearsal ran eight exchanges in no fixed order, each answer written into the sketchbook before the next question. Twice the conductor said it was nearly ready and named the one thing missing. The third time it showed the concert as a picture in three batches and asked for the go.

Players worked in parallel, and every return was read against its part of the score. What went wrong was caught by somebody reading or rendering the real thing: install commands that did not exist, an invented eighth step, picture frames that never filled. Readers who had never seen Kerd found what the pages left out; two steps were performed again, and new readers read the result.

What the record does not show
Whether real people find any of it useful. That cannot be read from inside the work.
