Good work. In concert.

You rehearse a piece of work with the AI until you both know what done means. Then a score is written, and a team of agents performs it, with your goals checked before it ends.

Nine seats. Each one is there because something goes wrong without it.

Point at a seat on the plan, or tab through them.

Centre front, on the podium.

Conductor.

/kerd:conductor

Without it
Done is whatever the AI says.
With it
Done is pinned down in rehearsal before anything is built, and before the concert ends the result is compared with your goals. A gap the score cannot answer comes back to you for that one point, not to the start.

In the hall. Best seat in the house.

You.

it’s your piece

Without you
There is no piece, only a chat that stops when it feels finished.
With you
You decide what done means and when rehearsal ends. Nothing is performed that you have not agreed.

In the wings, where the running order lives.

Stage manager.

/kerd:switch

Without it
Every new chat starts from nothing, and you re-explain.
With it
You sit down and it tells you where things stand, what is open, and the one thing it would do next and why. On this machine or another.

At the easel, upstage.

Illustrator.

/kerd:visuals

Without it
You agree to words you can’t picture.
With it
A proposal with connected parts, a branch or a before and after arrives with a picture, saved and rendered. Nobody asks whether you would like a diagram.

At the desk at the back. Finishes before anyone plays.

Composer.

writes the score

Without it
The AI builds before anyone knows what done is.
With it
When you say you are ready, what rehearsal settled is written as a score: the plan, one step per part. The asking is over before the playing starts.

In the arcs, side by side.

Players.

agents, side by side

Without them
One chat plays every part, one at a time.
With them
Several agents at once, each given one step and running alongside the others, and every return checked before it counts.

In the box. A clear view, and no stake in the build.

Second opinion.

/kerd:agent, Codex or Claude

Without it
The AI marks its own homework.
With it
Another Claude or Codex session reads the work, given the brief and the sources, not the builder’s account of what it did. A model that has not spent the last hour convincing itself.

Backstage, with the tuning fork.

Tuner.

/kerd:tend, /kerd:slainte

Without it
The project drifts out of tune.
With it
Tend checks the repo’s structure against current convention. Slainte reports where a doc’s claims no longer match the code, with the evidence that found each one.

Backstage, by the shelves.

Librarian.

/kerd:kivna

Without it
What you learn leaves with the chat.
With it
What you learn goes into an Obsidian vault in plain words, updated in place rather than piled up in dated logs.

One piece of work, from first rehearsal to the last bow.

It starts in rehearsal. You have a piece of work and a rough idea of done. You and the conductor work it through turn by turn, in any order, and you keep shipping as you go. Nobody performs anything yet. This is the part where you find out what you meant.

You and the conductor, centre front.

What gets settled goes in the sketchbook. One sketchbook per piece of work, a running note of what is agreed. Ask “where are we?” at any point and the answer comes from the sketchbook, not from whatever the chat happens to remember. When a proposal has parts that connect, the illustrator draws it, so you agree to a picture rather than a paragraph.

The illustrator, at the easel.

Rehearsal rarely fits in one sitting. Stop when you like. The stage manager writes down your place, and when you sit down again, here or on another machine, it tells you where things stand, what is open, and the one thing it would do next and why. Nobody re-explains anything.

The stage manager, in the wings, with your place.

“I think we’re ready.” Only you get that line. Rehearsal ends when you decide it does, and nothing is performed that you have not agreed.

You, from the hall.

The composer writes the score. What rehearsal settled becomes the plan, one step per part. From here there are no turn-by-turn questions, because the asking already happened.

The composer, at the desk.

The players perform it, side by side. Each player is an agent given one step, running alongside the others, and the conductor checks every return before it counts. If something turns up that the score cannot answer, that part stops and comes back to you for that one point, not to the start.

Enter the players. The conductor raises the baton.

A second opinion reads the work. Another Claude or Codex session: a partner you have already paired with, which knows the project, or a fresh reviewer started for that one job. It gets the brief and the sources, not the builder’s account of how well it went.

The second opinion, in the box.

The goals are checked before it ends. The result is compared with the goals you set in rehearsal. Done is when your spec and goals are met, which is a different moment from when the AI feels finished.

The conductor turns to face the hall.

After the bow, backstage. The librarian keeps what was learned, in plain words. The tuner checks the project has not drifted: structure, stale counts, docs that no longer match the code. The stage manager writes down where things stand, so the next sitting starts there.

House lights up.

Take your seat.

Two commands in Claude Code. The concert starts when you say so, not when it installs.

Add the marketplace, then the plugin. For Codex, which takes a different route, see getting started.

claude plugin marketplace add anthonymaley/Kerd
claude plugin install kerd@kerd-marketplace

Then sit down: /kerd:switch in, answer its question, do the work, /kerd:switch out.
