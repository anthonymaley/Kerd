# Launch story — when Model-ready work succeeds

value is understandable. This is not a prediction or a claim about shipped

## PEOPLE STOP WRITING PROMPTS AND START DESCRIBING RESULTS

### Kerd's new Model-ready work system promises faster, higher-quality AI work without making people supervise the machinery

**NEW YORK —** Product leaders, project teams, executives, researchers,
creators, operators, and software teams have spent years learning an awkward
new job: explaining work to artificial intelligence systems in exactly the right way.
Today Kerd introduced Model-ready work, a system designed to make that the
software's responsibility instead.

A person begins by describing what they want to create, decide, understand, or
change in ordinary language. Kerd's
Conductor asks a short series of focused questions, retrieves what the project
already knows, and creates a brief showing the outcome, proof, boundaries, and
decisions that still belong to the person. It then prepares that work
differently for the selected Claude or OpenAI model.

The underlying requirement remains unchanged. The model receives instructions,
context, examples, tools, and an effort level suited to its own model family.
The result is judged against the original measures rather than whether the
model followed a prescribed sequence.

“The user should define the destination, not learn the engine,” the product
team said in its design statement.

The system has three proposed work levels. Quick handles narrow, reversible
changes with mechanical proof. Standard handles ordinary product features.
Important/Deep adds recovery planning, independent review, and explicit
approval for consequential or difficult-to-reverse work.

The most visible difference may be what users do not have to read. Instead of
long planning documents, the default view answers five questions: what are we
building, where are we, what is happening now, what has been proven, and what
needs a decision. People can move from that product view into flow,
architecture, detailed sequences, and full evidence when their decision needs
more depth.

Kerd also avoids open-ended conversations between models. When multiple models
can help, each receives a bounded job: execute, review, investigate one
uncertainty, or produce a competing solution. Conductor combines the evidence.
Agreement between models is not treated as proof.

The same pattern can support a product proposal, research synthesis, operating
plan, customer journey, launch, analysis, document, presentation, or software
feature. The work and proof differ; the person should not have to become an AI
specialist for any of them.

The company says speed should come from better preparation rather than weaker
standards: fewer questions the repository can already answer, focused context,
the least costly model proven for the work, parallel independent jobs, and
verification tied to measurable outcomes.

Model-ready work launches first as a measured trial beside Kerd's existing
Conductor. The company will compare task success, defects, proof completeness,
time, cost, avoidable questions, interruptions, and rework before replacing the
current workflow.

The larger wager is simple: if models are allowed to work in the way they work
best, while people retain control of outcomes and consequential decisions, AI
software may become both easier to use and more trustworthy.
